Digital Forensics

Investigating Cyber Incidents and Collecting Digital Evidence

What is Digital Forensics?

Digital Forensics is the process of identifying, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence from electronic devices. It supports cybersecurity investigations and legal proceedings.

Types of Digital Forensics

  • Computer Forensics: Analyzing computers and storage devices
  • Mobile Device Forensics: Investigating smartphones and tablets
  • Network Forensics: Monitoring and analyzing network traffic
  • Cloud Forensics: Examining cloud storage and services
  • Memory Forensics: Investigating volatile memory (RAM)
  • Malware Forensics: Analyzing malicious software

Digital Forensics Process

  • Identification: Detect devices or data relevant to the investigation
  • Preservation: Prevent alteration of digital evidence
  • Collection: Acquire data from devices securely
  • Examination: Analyze data to uncover relevant evidence
  • Analysis: Interpret findings and reconstruct events
  • Reporting: Document evidence, methods, and conclusions for legal or organizational use

Popular Digital Forensics Tools

  • Autopsy / Sleuth Kit – Disk and file system analysis
  • FTK (Forensic Toolkit) – Comprehensive forensic suite
  • EnCase – Digital investigation and evidence management
  • Wireshark – Network traffic analysis
  • Volatility – Memory forensics
  • Cellebrite – Mobile device forensics

Best Practices in Digital Forensics

  • Maintain chain of custody for evidence
  • Use write-blockers when acquiring storage devices
  • Document all steps and findings in detail
  • Regularly update forensic tools and training
  • Ensure compliance with legal and organizational policies
